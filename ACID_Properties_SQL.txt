ACID properties in SQL
-------------------------

Imagine you are using a banking app to send ₹500 to your friend. Two things must happen:

1. ₹500 is subtracted from your account.
2. ₹500 is added to your friend's account.
What happens if the bank's internet crashes right after step 1? Your money disappears into thin air!
To prevent nightmares like this, databases use a set of safety rules called ACID properties. ACID ensures that every transaction (a group of SQL actions) is completely safe, reliable, and error-free.
Here is the breakdown of what ACID stands for, explained simply:

1. Atomicity (All or Nothing)

Think of a transaction like an atom—it cannot be split up.

> Either everything in your request succeeds, or nothing happens at all.
> In our bank example: If the system crashes halfway through, the database cancels the whole thing. Your ₹500 is safely put back into your account as if nothing ever started.

2. Consistency (Follow the Rules)

The database must always follow strict data rules before and after a change. It cannot break its own logic.
> In our bank example: If your account cannot have a negative balance, the database will automatically block a transaction that tries to send more money than you actually have. The data always remains valid.

3. Isolation (Don't Disturb Others)

In the real world, millions of people use the same database at the exact same time. Isolation ensures that simultaneous transactions do not mess with each other.
> In our bank example: If you and your dad both try to withdraw money from the same account at the exact same second, the database forces them to wait in a tiny virtual line. Transaction B won't see what Transaction A is doing until Transaction A is fully finished.

4. Durability (Built to Last)

Once a transaction is successful, it is written in stone.
> In our bank example: Once you see the "Success" screen, even if the bank's power goes out or the server explodes a second later, your data is completely safe. The database saves it to a permanent hard drive so it will never be lost or forgotten.

Quick Summary Table
--------------------------

Atomicity -	100% complete or 0% complete. No halfway.
Consistency	- Data must obey all rules and numbers must add up.
Isolation -	Transactions mind their own business and don't overlap.
Durability - Once saved, it is permanent and cannot be lost.
