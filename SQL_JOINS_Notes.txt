
Imp Note
---------

When you write just:

JOIN

it does not mean "any type of join." It specifically means INNER JOIN.

For example, these two queries are identical:

SELECT *
FROM Employee e
JOIN Department d
ON e.dept_id = d.dept_id;

SELECT *
FROM Employee e
INNER JOIN Department d
ON e.dept_id = d.dept_id;

JOIN is simply the short form of INNER JOIN.

For other types, you must write them explicitly.

There is no separate SELF JOIN keyword in SQL.

A self join is simply a normal join where the same table appears twice using aliases.

FROM Employee e
JOIN Employee m
ON e.manager_id = m.emp_id;

Here, JOIN still means INNER JOIN—it's called a self join only because both sides of the join are the same table.

JOIN is the shorthand for INNER JOIN. It returns only the rows that have matching values in both tables.

Think of JOIN as a family:
----------------------------

JOINs
│
├── INNER JOIN   <- "JOIN" is shorthand for this
├── LEFT JOIN
├── RIGHT JOIN
├── FULL OUTER JOIN
├── CROSS JOIN
└── SELF JOIN (a technique, not a keyword)
